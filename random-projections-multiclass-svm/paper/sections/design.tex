\section{The extension: design}
\label{sec:design}

The extension asks whether sparse random projections preserve class structure as well as dense ones, and how that depends on the target size $k$.

\emph{Grid.} The grid uses the five matrix families of Section~\ref{sec:background}, plus two references: all features, and PCA to $k$ dimensions fit on the training rows. The target sizes are $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24\}$ with $k < d$: 1 to 3 for Iris, 1 to 6 for Seeds, 1 to 8 for Glass, 1 to 12 for Wine, and 1 to 24 for Breast Cancer. The very sparse family is not run on Glass, where $s = \sqrt{d} = 3$ makes it Eq.~4. Columns are nested: $k$ uses the first $k$ columns of one $d \times 24$ base matrix. Each setting has 100 draws, and draw $r$ uses a stratified 70/30 split with seed $r$ and its own matrices. Every family, baseline, and kernel in a draw shares the split, so differences are paired. In all, there are \N{z:n_rows} classification rows.

\emph{Pipeline.} Per draw: scale each feature to $[0, 1]$ on the training rows, project, standardize, tune each kernel by 3-fold stratified cross-validation on the training rows, refit, and score the 30\% test rows. The SVMs are scikit-learn's, built on LIBSVM \cite{pedregosa2011,chang2011}. The tuning grids are $C \in \{0.01, \dots, 100\}$ for the linear kernel, $C$ and $\gamma$ for the polynomial kernels (with offset 1) and the RBF kernel, 133 fits for each draw in each cell. The headline model is the kernel with the best inner cross-validation accuracy (ties go to the earlier kernel in the order linear, degree 2, degree 3, and RBF), never the best test score.

\emph{Statistics.} Intervals are 95\% percentile bootstrap intervals \cite{efron1993} of the mean over the 100 draws. The primary comparisons are the cross-validated model's test accuracy (balanced accuracy for Glass) for Eq.~3, Eq.~4, very sparse, and one per column against the Gaussian, and Eq.~4 against Eq.~3, at every (data set, $k$): \N{c:n_primary} comparisons, with paired $t$-tests adjusted by Holm's method \cite{holm1979} at the 0.05 level. A comparison is called equivalent when its 95\% interval lies inside $\pm 2$ points. Because cells are nested across $k$ and share draws, counts of cells are not independent evidence. A pooled test (the mean difference over a data set's $k$ values, averaged over data sets, per draw, then a one-sample $t$-test over draws) is used for the overall deficit.
