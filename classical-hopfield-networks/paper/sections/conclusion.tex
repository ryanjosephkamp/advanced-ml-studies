\section{Conclusion}

With correct weights, the assignment's Hopfield network still recalls none of the 18 dense character bitmaps: every query falls into one spurious state. On random patterns, Hebbian recall collapses quickly as patterns overlap: the load at which exact recall falls to 50\% drops from \N{c:proto:0.0:hebb:10} for independent patterns to \N{c:proto:0.4:hebb:10} at an expected overlap of 0.4 (10\% noise, 144 neurons, interpolated between tested loads), and at low overlap, the assignment's 0/1 update lowers it further. On patterns that share a prototype, ranked by capacity from lowest to highest wherever their capacities can be told apart, the rules run: Hebbian and covariance, Storkey, the pseudoinverse rule, and the modern network at $\beta = 64$, which on the characters behaves as a nearest-pattern lookup; at $\beta = 4$, the modern network falls below Storkey at expected overlaps of 0.1 to 0.6. Density, shared structure beyond density, and, for the assignment's update, load each break recall on their own. The characters have all three, the shared structure and uneven densities likely also defeat the covariance rule, and the study cannot say how much each contributes.
